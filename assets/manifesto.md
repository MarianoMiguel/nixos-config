# MANIFESTO

---

When you wake, remember what you are getting up for.

When you read this, remember the fire in your heart when you wrote it.

You have a family now. Your life is not only your own.

Live in a way that makes your daughter proud to say, **“That is my dad.”**  \
Live in a way that makes your wife proud of the man she chose as her husband.  \
Live in a way that lets your mother and father look at the man you became and feel proud of the person they raised.

Not because you need applause from them, but because you want your life to be worthy of the love, trust, and responsibility they have placed in you.

**Do what needs to be done.**
The dishes, the cleaning, the errands, the repairs, the small obligations nobody applauds. It does not matter whether you feel like doing them. A home is built and maintained by people who notice what needs doing and do it.

**Get strong.**
Train even when you would rather not. Lift. Move. Condition your body. Strength is useful, and you have people you want to be capable of helping and protecting.

**Learn to be dangerous, and remain disciplined enough that it matters.**
Practice BJJ. Box. Learn to defend yourself and your family. Competence is better than bravado. Stay calm, capable, and controlled.

**Stay healthy.**
Eat food that serves you instead of whatever feels good for ten minutes. Sleep. Go to your checkups. Pay attention when something is wrong. Your wife and daughter do not merely need you today; they need you decades from now. Do what you can to arrive there healthy.

**Stay sharp.**
Read. Think. Question things. Learn how the world works. Do not outsource your judgment. You are one of the filters between your family and bad decisions, bad information, manipulation, and stupidity. Keep that filter sharp.

**Focus.**
When it is time to work, work. Put the phone away. Close the distractions. Do the difficult thing in front of you instead of escaping into easier things. A scattered hour repeated for years becomes a scattered life.

**Do the work.**
Never take your ability to provide for granted. Your work pays for the food, the house, the safety, the opportunities, and the future of the people you love. Keep learning. Keep becoming more useful. Never become complacent because things happen to be going well today.

**Respect money.**
You do not need to prove that you have money by spending it. Save. Invest. Avoid stupid debt. Buy deliberately. Remember that every unnecessary expense competes with freedom, security, and the future you are building for your family.

**Stay disciplined.**
Your daughter will learn far more from what she watches you do than from what you tell her to do. Let her see someone who keeps his word, controls his impulses, takes responsibility, works hard, admits when he is wrong, and does unpleasant things without complaining.

**Learn to cook.**
Feed the people you love. Make the ordinary evening meal one of the ways they know they are cared for.

You will not always be motivated. That is irrelevant.

You do not clean because you are motivated to clean.  \
You do not train because you are motivated to train.  \
You do not eat well because you happen to crave healthy food.  \
You do not work because every morning feels inspiring.  \
You do not save money because spending nothing is exciting.

You do these things because they are yours to do.

So get out of bed.

Take care of your body.  \
Take care of your mind.  \
Take care of your work.  \
Take care of your money.  \
Take care of your home.  \
Take care of your people.

Give your daughter a father she can admire before she is old enough to understand why.

Give your wife a husband she can trust, respect, and be proud to stand beside.

Give your parents the satisfaction of seeing that everything they poured into you became something good.


Then tomorrow, get up and do it again.
