Interstellar

The system is Interstellar: bar, island, dock, sidebar, Settings, the lock, login and disk-unlock screens. A theme is everything it looks and sounds like: palette, type, shape, material, motion, wallpaper. Folio stays exactly as it is, inks and plates included, as one theme. Lumen is the second: neutral grays, plain sans-serif labels, frosted glass. Each theme takes one color of your choosing; the rest of the palette belongs to the theme.

The desktop

One system, two themes

The same desktop in whichever theme, mode and color the bar below picks. The Settings window works: choose a theme, mode or color there too.

Disk unlock

The theme reaches the disk-unlock screen

The passphrase screen runs before the disk is unlocked, so it cannot read your settings from your home folder. Instead, a small system service copies the look onto the boot partition, the same way Chrome already gets its color today. The split stays the system's in every theme: the theme's art on the left, the page on the right, as on the lock screen. Shown here in the current mode and color.

You chooseSettings writes theme, mode and color to your look file, at once.
The system checksA root service reads three values only: a known theme, light or dark, one #rrggbb color. Anything else is refused.
It draws the screenFrom the system's own copy of the theme, never from files in your home, and saves it beside the kernel.
Next bootThe unlock screen, login and lock all match. No rebuild, no password prompt.
Your color

Any color, kept readable

Pick any color for the accent. Interstellar keeps its hue and moves its lightness until text and controls stay readable: deeper on paper, lighter at night. Backgrounds, text and the terminal's other colors stay the theme's. Free choice there is where desktops become unreadable, and every app theme is generated from that palette.
